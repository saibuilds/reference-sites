— The Story of the House —

Mineola East is one of those neighbourhoods you forget is half an hour from Bay Street. The streets run on a slow grade down to the lake. The trees were here before the houses.

1234 Mineola Avenue sits on a corner where two streets meet at a soft angle and a horse-chestnut leans over the boulevard. The lot is ninety by two hundred, the rear faces south, and the grade falls away gently enough that the back garden gets a view of nothing but treetops. The original 1962 bungalow was taken down to the foundations in 2017 and rebuilt as a centre-hall Georgian by a partner at a downtown firm who wanted his children to grow up on a street where they could ride bikes without being watched.

We were brought in by the second owner — a senior partner at a tax practice whose own children are now in university and whose move to Burlington had been planned for two years. The house had been maintained to the same standard as the day it was finished. The kitchen counters were unmarked. The mudroom had eight pairs of boots in graduated sizes. The garden had a peony bed that the seller's wife had nursed for nine seasons.

Our listing took fourteen weeks. We refused to rush the spring market. Photography waited until the peonies opened in late May. We hosted two private viewings — one for an agent who had a buyer in Forest Hill who was tired of the Forest Hill commute, one for a London-based family relocating to a Toronto law firm. We then opened the door for a single weekend.

Three written offers came in by the Sunday deadline. The accepted bid was just under ask, fully unconditional, with a sixty-day close to accommodate the seller's move and the buyer's children's school year. The peony bed transferred with the house, on the buyer's specific written request.
