— The Story of the Estate —

Some houses are never for sale. Some houses are only ever for the right buyer. 88 Russell Hill Road is the second kind.

The estate was built in 1928 for a Bay Street barrister who wanted a country house inside the city. The architect was Sproatt & Rolph, who two years earlier had drawn the Hart House library. The plaster ceilings were cast in the Don Valley. The millwork — the panelling in the library, the carved newel post on the principal stair, the leaded glass over the morning room — came from a workshop in Hamilton that closed in 1934 and was never replaced.

The current owner is the granddaughter of the original. She was born in the principal bedroom and has lived in the house, on and off, for sixty-one years. In 2019 she undertook a sympathetic restoration with a Toronto architect who specialised in Edwardian houses. The brief was that no surface installed before 1934 was to be touched, only stabilised. The Hamilton millwork was hand-cleaned with cotton swabs over the course of eleven months.

Our mandate is unusual. The owner has no intention of selling and no need of the proceeds. Her instruction is that we maintain a current private valuation, that we vet three off-market introductions per calendar year, and that no photograph of the interior leaves the firm's archive. The two of us are the only agents in Toronto who have walked the principal floor since 2019. The price you see is indicative, not advertised.

When the house does eventually change hands, it will be by private treaty, to a buyer who has accepted the heritage covenant on the millwork, the plaster, and the walled rose garden at the rear. We expect that buyer to be a single family. We expect the introduction to take longer than the transaction.
