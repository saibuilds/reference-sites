— The Story of the House —

Twelve-thousand square feet of restraint. Limestone on three sides, oak through the centre, and a back garden that the morning fog hasn't given up on in forty years.

Park Lane Circle is the kind of street where the houses talk to each other in whole sentences. Number twenty-three was built in 1986, expanded in 2002, and quietly reworked in 2019 by an architect who understood that the most expensive thing in the Bridle Path isn't square footage — it's the gap between the houses, the depth of the canopy, the way light arrives in the front hall at four in the afternoon in November. We took the listing in February with a brief from the family that read, almost word for word: please don't let it be photographed badly.

We spent six weeks before listing. The stager pulled three rooms apart and rebuilt them around two paintings the family had been keeping in storage. We re-lit the limestone facade with three temporary fixtures so the evening photographs would carry the right shadow across the cornice. The garden was edited — not pruned, edited — by a designer who understood that an estate sale begins at the property line. We held a single off-market preview for seven advisors we trust. By the time the listing hit MLS, three of those advisors had brought their clients back for a second look.

The interior choices are quiet on purpose. White oak quartersawn through the principal rooms, honed Carrara on the kitchen island, brushed brass hardware on doors that haven't been touched since the original build. The lower level — a wine cellar, a screening room, a quiet office with windows onto the cedar court — is the part of the house that surprised us most. It is not a basement. It is the part of the house where the family actually lived.

We list houses like this the way we'd list a piece of furniture — with provenance, with patience, and with a careful refusal to over-explain. Number twenty-three deserves the buyer who walks in and immediately stops talking. We expect to find that buyer before the spring market crowds the front lawn.
