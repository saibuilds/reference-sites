— The Story of the Suite —

Penthouse one rides above the avenue at exactly the right height — high enough to forget the traffic, low enough to still hear the cathedral bells from St. Paul's on Sunday morning.

The owners had lived in the suite for eleven years and were the second family to do so. Their brief was almost an apology: please find someone who will love the south terrace the way we did. The penthouse runs the full floor plate of the tower — three bedrooms wrapped around a great-room that opens to a 1,200 sqft terrace facing south over Yorkville's low rooflines. North light from the kitchen, west sunsets from the principal bath. We have never staged a suite that asked for less.

We brought in two pieces — a long oak dining table and a single Knoll lounge chair in cognac leather — and otherwise let the architecture do the talking. The original 2014 finishes are honest: rift oak floors, white-painted millwork, a single block of Cremo Delicato as the kitchen island. The terrace was edited with three planters of olive, lemon, and bay, supplied by a nursery in Mississauga who has been growing potted trees for Yorkville suites for a decade.

We held one weekend of off-market viewings. Two buyers, both already in the building. The successful party was a couple from London who had been searching for a Toronto pied-a-terre for two years and had walked through eight penthouses without an offer. They closed at full ask, no conditions, three-month leaseback to give the sellers time to settle into the home they were moving to in Lawrence Park.

This is what a tower sale looks like when no one is in a hurry: short list of buyers, long list of reasons. The successful party first sat on the terrace at 5:14pm on a Saturday in March, watched the sun drop behind the CN Tower, and asked us for a pen.
