— The Founder —

Sai Nathan, broker of record.

SathiDeals was started in 2019 from a kitchen table on Avenue Road. Sai Nathan, the founder, had spent the previous decade as a residential agent at a large downtown brokerage, closing four hundred and twelve transactions and learning — quietly, after the third or fourth nine-figure listing — that the best work in Toronto real estate was the work no one ever read about in the paper.

He left to build a smaller practice. The brief was unusual: no signs on lawns, no postcards through doors, no monthly newsletter, no quarterly market report. Instead, a roster capped at sixty-five families at any given time, each carried for the long arc of two or three generations of moves. Some sales would be off-market. Some would be advisory only. Some would be houses we agreed never to sell at all.

The studio is run today by Sai and his sister Sathi, with one full-time associate and a small bench of vetted counsel, surveyors, heritage consultants and quiet lenders. The work is split roughly evenly between Forest Hill, Rosedale, Lawrence Park, the Bridle Path, and a handful of midtown blocks where families have lived for thirty years and intend to live for thirty more.

Sai was born in Scarborough, raised between an aunt's house in Don Mills and his father's first rental property in East York, and has not lived more than nine kilometres from the corner of Yonge and Eglinton in his entire adult life. He believes, with some evidence, that a Toronto neighbourhood is a slower instrument than the index that tracks it, and that the right house, held for long enough, becomes the second portrait of a family.

— What We Stand For —

Four principles, written down once.

I
The conversation is the gift.
We listen for forty minutes before we say a word about price. The transaction, if it ever comes, is almost incidental.
II
Hold the file for a decade.
Closing day is the start of a relationship, not the end of a contract. We are the people you will text about the roofer in 2031.
III
Write everything by hand, twice.
Listing copy, offer letters, neighbourhood notes. If a sentence is not worth writing twice, it is not worth sending.
IV
Tell the truth, even when it costs us.
If your next move does not make sense, we will say so — and you will have lost nothing but an evening with strangers who turned out to be useful.
