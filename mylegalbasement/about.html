— The Founder —

Sai Nathan, permit lead.

I grew up in a Scarborough house where my parents quietly rented the basement to a graduate student named Ravi. That second income paid the mortgage faster than two adult salaries could. I watched a family build wealth from a hallway and a separate side door, and I never forgot it.

I started MyLegalBasement in 2019 after twelve years of swinging a hammer on other people's renovations. The brief was simple. Toronto has roughly eight hundred thousand single-family lots. Most of them are sitting on a second home they have never met — a basement that needs egress and a side entrance, or a backyard that can hold a 645 sqft garden suite under the city's bylaw. My job is to find which one your lot holds, file the permits, supervise the build, and place a tenant before the dust settles.

What I am not is a franchise. There is no subcontracted sales team, no call centre, no quarterly leadership offsite. The crew is six carpenters, two electricians, and a permit coordinator I have worked with for a decade. We do about fifteen builds a year. I am at every site survey. I am on every Saturday call. When the city plan examiner has a question on your drawings in week four, I am the person who answers the email.

I live in East York with my partner and two kids. We do not work in cities I do not personally know. If your house is between Lake Ontario and Steeles, between Etobicoke Creek and the Rouge, we will probably know your block. If we do not, we will say so on the first call.

— What We Stand For —

Four house rules, written on the shop wall.

I
Permit first, paint last.
If it cannot be permitted, we will not build it. A legal second suite is the difference between a Sunday afternoon nuisance and a thirty-year asset on the title.
II
One number, one builder.
No call centre. No subcontracted sales team. The phone you call on a Saturday is the phone of the person who supervised your build.
III
Tell you the number on day one.
Audits are free. The estimate that comes out of the audit is the price. We do not write change orders we have not first discussed at the kitchen table.
IV
A tenant before we leave the site.
Every project ends with a vetted lease in your hand. The rent starts the week after we hand back the keys.
