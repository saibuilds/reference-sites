— The Story of the Suite —

A slope lot in East York is the secret of a walk-out basement. The grade does the work; the suite gets full daylight without underpinning.

1442 Coxwell is a 1948 detached with a generous rear setback and a lot that falls four feet from front to back. The previous owner had finished the basement himself in 2003 with carpet and drop-ceiling, used it for a teenager, and abandoned it when the teenager left. The new owners — a paramedic and a hospital nurse, both shift workers — bought the house at the bottom of the 2022 dip and asked us to make the basement a proper second suite.

The walk-out was already half-there. A single concrete step led from the rear door to grade. We cut a full patio door into the same opening, rebuilt the threshold, poured a small flush patio outside, and re-graded the rear lawn so the door sat at exact lawn level. From the kitchen window upstairs, you cannot see that the basement has its own front door. From the lawn, you can.

Inside is a long, wide rectangle: two bedrooms at the south end with egress windows, a bathroom in the middle, and a living-kitchen at the north against the walk-out door. The kitchen is a flat-front IKEA carcass with a hand-built oak face frame, a 24-inch range, and a soapstone-look quartz counter. The bedrooms have wool-blend carpet. The living strip has the same pale-oak vinyl plank as the upstairs main floor.

The first tenant was a couple in their late twenties who had been renting a one-bedroom in Riverdale and wanted out of the third-floor walk-up. They walked the suite on a Sunday afternoon, signed a lease on the Tuesday, and moved in on the first of the following month. The owners use the rent to pay the variable-rate portion of the mortgage. They sleep slightly better than they used to.
