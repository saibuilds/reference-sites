— The Story of the Suite —

The Beaches is a neighbourhood of narrow lots, mature silver maples, and a boardwalk that still smells of lake water and fried dough on a Saturday afternoon.

144 Queen Street East is a 1924 brick semi on a 25-foot lot that runs almost two hundred feet back to a public laneway. The owners bought it in 2003 for a price that now reads as a typographical error. They raised one daughter in the upstairs bedroom and never quite needed the back third of the property. When their daughter moved to Halifax in 2024, they called us about the long strip of grass behind the garage.

The garden-suite bylaw, passed in 2022, made the back of the lot eligible for a detached one-storey home up to six hundred square feet. The constraint we worked against was the silver maple at the centre of the yard, sixty years old, a protected tree under the city's bylaw with a critical root zone that swallowed the eastern half of the buildable envelope. The studio sits in the western half of what remained, set back from the maple by four metres of pea gravel and a low cedar bench that doubles as a tree-protection barrier.

The envelope is cedar shiplap on three sides and a single south-facing window wall on the fourth. The roof is a shallow shed pitched toward the laneway with a continuous gutter that drains into a buried rain barrel. Inside, the studio is one room with a Murphy bed on the long wall, a galley kitchen along the short wall, and a three-piece bath behind a sliding pocket door. The floor is polished concrete with embedded radiant tubes. The ceiling is the underside of the roof deck, painted white. Everything else is cedar.

The first tenant is a watercolour painter in her early thirties who teaches a Saturday class at the Gardiner Museum. She uses the window wall as her studio and the Murphy bed as her bed and tells us the silver maple is, in her words, the best northern shade tree she has ever painted. The owners use the rent to take a slow train across the country every September.
