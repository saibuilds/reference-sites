— The Story of the Suite —

The laneway is the missing front door of the modern Toronto lot. We treat it that way: a real address, a real entrance, a real garden.

88 Yorkminster Road sits on one of the original Willowdale 50-foot lots, a 1962 sidesplit with a single-car garage at the rear that opens onto a paved laneway. The owners, retired, raised three children in the house. The youngest left in 2022, the second car left with her, and the garage became a place where racquets and lawn chairs went to die. They asked us whether the garage could become a small place to live.

The garage came down in week ten. The new structure occupies exactly the same footprint — 16 by 40 feet — with a quarter of that footprint kept as a covered laneway parking pad. The suite itself is 640 square feet on a single storey: a galley kitchen against the long west wall, a bedroom and a 3-piece bath at the south end, and an open living strip at the north with a window facing the back of the main house. The laneway elevation has a single door, a sconce, and a brushed-brass house number that reads 88-A.

The exterior is hand-painted vertical fir in a deep, warm green, chosen to match the cedar hedge that the owners planted along the lot line in 1985. The interior is the opposite — Decorator's White walls, white-washed oak floor, painted millwork — so that the compact footprint reads as bright. A clerestory window over the bed pulls morning light from the east; a 6-foot patio door opens from the living strip onto a small flagstone patio with a single linden tree.

The first tenant was a clinical psychologist in private practice who saw the suite on the day the city signed the final inspection and wanted to know if the linden tree was part of the lease. She signed a one-year on the spot. The owners spent the rent cheque on a trip to Italy. The psychologist sits under the linden tree on Saturday mornings with the laneway door propped open.
