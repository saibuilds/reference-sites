— The Story of the Suite —

Mineola East is the part of Mississauga that still reads as a small town, with hedgerow streets, hundred-foot lots, and a CN line a block south that you hear only when the wind is right.

The clients bought 1234 Mineola Avenue East in 1991, raised three sons in it, and have watched the lot quietly become more valuable than the house. The mother of the family is eighty-one. She still cooks every Sunday for whichever children and grandchildren happen to be in town, but the stairs to the second floor have started to feel longer each year. The family asked us, in the spring of 2024, whether we could build her a small bungalow at the back of the property so she could keep her independence without leaving the street she had lived on for thirty-three years.

Mississauga's garden-suite framework, finalised in 2023, allows a detached one-storey accessory dwelling up to seven hundred and seventy-six square feet on a lot the size of theirs. We worked to a hard seven hundred to keep below the threshold for a second servicing connection, sharing the existing water and sanitary laterals with a backflow preventer and a separate hydro meter. The Committee of Adjustment hearing took one evening; the neighbour two doors down brought a tray of dates and stayed for the vote.

The form is a square plan with a low hipped roof, a small covered porch facing the main house, and lap siding painted the same soft warm white as the original 1956 bungalow. From the street the suite is invisible. From the main house's kitchen window it reads, deliberately, as a small companion building — the same proportion as a 1950s detached garage, the same fascia depth, the same eight-over-one window pattern. Inside, the bedroom is at the quiet north end, the kitchen and sitting room open south through a wide casement onto the shared garden, and the bathroom is a full curbless wet room with grab-blocking everywhere it might one day be needed.

The mother moved in on a Saturday in the autumn. Her grandchildren walked her down the path between the maple and the magnolia, carrying her chair and a single suitcase. The Sunday dinner is now hosted in the main house, as it always was, but she walks home through the garden when she is tired, and her bedroom light goes on shortly after.
