— The Story of the Suite —

Mimico Village still has the platform clock, the bakery, the lake at the end of the street. A basement suite here pays a mortgage and feeds a neighbourhood.

23 Mimico Avenue is a 1929 stucco bungalow three minutes from Mimico GO. The owners bought it in the spring of 2019, paid too much by their own admission, and discovered eight months later that one of them was about to start working from home permanently and the other was about to be on parental leave. The basement was a single open room with a furnace and a workshop, ceilings at six feet four, one small hopper window at the front.

We underpinned to seven feet six, dug down two more courses than the engineer asked for, and re-poured the slab with a sleeper sub-floor so the finished ceiling cleared seven feet eight. The new side entrance was the harder problem — the lot is narrow and the side yard slopes toward the foundation — so we cut a 4-foot-wide stairwell with a poured retaining wall, a galvanized handrail, and a small canopy hung off the stucco above. The retaining wall is the only thing the tenant sees from the sidewalk; we treated it as a front door, not a basement hatch.

The interior is plain. One bedroom at the rear under a new egress window, a 4-piece bath with a soaker tub, an open kitchen-and-living strip along the front wall, and a small mechanical closet that holds a tankless and a heat-pump head. The floor is engineered oak, the cabinets are flat-panel maple in a warm clear coat, the counter is a single 8-foot piece of paper-composite the colour of unbleached linen. Nothing tries hard. Everything is calm.

The first tenant was a CN crew scheduler on a four-on-three rotation, found by the owners through a colleague at work, signed before the final inspection. He keeps a single bike in the stairwell and a yucca on the kitchen counter. The owners say the cheque covers the mortgage, the property tax, and a kid's worth of daycare, with a few hundred left over for the bakery up the street.
