Mississauga · Wine Cellar

A compact 900-bottle cellar — cedar racking, climate control, brass label rails and a low-temp LED grazing wash for the magnums.
MISSISSAUGA, ONTARIOCELLAR · 110 SFCEDAR + LED · 5 WK
— The Particulars —

A small room, built for thirty years of holding.

Client
A licensed wine importer carving 110 sf out of a Mineola basement for his working library
Location
Mineola, Mississauga, west of the QEW
Scope
110 sqft sealed cellar · 900-bottle capacity · ducted split + dual-zone monitoring
Completion
5-week insert build · vapour barrier · tasting ledge with brass label rails
Investment
$58,000 turnkey including the climate unit and the cabinetwork
Team
DJ crew · Designer: in-house · Climate: WineGuardian · Lighting: Tre Ci
— The Story of the Build —

The client imports allocation Burgundy and Mosel Riesling for a small private list. He had been storing both, badly, in a coat closet beside the laundry room.

The site was a section of finished basement directly under the dining-room above. We had a hard fourteen-foot run between the chimney chase and the mechanical room, eight-foot ceilings, and a single existing supply register we were happy to abandon. The brief was straightforward: hold roughly nine hundred bottles — six hundred standards, two hundred halves, fifty magnums and a small selection of larger formats — at fifty-five degrees and seventy per cent relative humidity, indefinitely.

The room is framed inside the existing finished space as a continuous vapour-barrier envelope. Two-inch closed-cell spray foam to the cold sides, six-mil poly on the warm side, taped and sealed at every penetration. The door is a solid-core slab with a full perimeter gasket and a drop seal at the threshold, hung on a cooled-temp-rated jamb. Behind the drywall on the warm-side walls we ran a dedicated condensate drain line for the split, gravity-pitched to the floor drain in the mechanical room next door.

The racking is western red cedar, dressed and oiled, built to a custom layout that mixes individual-bottle bays for the working library with diamond bins for back-vintages, half-bottle ladders for the halves, and a single deep magnum cradle at the bottom of the left-hand wall. A walnut-topped tasting ledge runs the back wall at counter height, with brass rails above for the chalk labels he uses to mark the row. Behind the ledge, a pair of small drawers hold a Coravin, two corkscrews and the temperature log he keeps by hand.

The lighting is the part he cares about most. Low-temperature LED tape under each racking shelf throws a grazing wash across the labels without putting heat into the room or photo-damaging the older corks. Three small recessed spots over the tasting ledge are warm-white and on a separate dimmer. He says he reads in there sometimes, when the upstairs is loud.

Load
