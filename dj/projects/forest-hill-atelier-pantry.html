— The Story of the Build —

The pantry sits between the kitchen and the dining room of a 1928 Forest Hill Georgian. It used to be a butler’s pantry. We were asked to bring back the butler.

The family of five returned to Toronto in the summer of 2024 after eleven years in London. They had grown used to a particular kind of English domestic plan in which the pantry is a working room of its own, not a stretch of cabinetry borrowed from the kitchen. The original Forest Hill Georgian had once held exactly that — a six-by-twelve room between the cooking kitchen and the formal dining room, with a sink, a counter, a window onto the garden, and a single swinging door at each end. A 1986 renovation had collapsed it into a passage. The brief was to reverse that, gently, without rebuilding the walls.

What we built is, technically, cabinetry. Eighteen linear feet of paint-grade inset casework along the long wall, with fluted glass upper doors, polished nickel bar pulls, and a continuous quartzite counter the colour of a late-October sky. What it does, however, is read as a room. The trick was the proportion: the upper cabinets stop sixteen inches below the ceiling and are crowned with a painted cove that meets the existing plaster cornice exactly. The bases sit on a recessed kick painted the same body colour as the wall, so the cabinets float just enough to read as furniture rather than millwork.

The middle three feet of the wall hold the integrated coffee station. A Miele plumbed unit slides behind a sectioned tambour door that retracts up and back into the cabinetry. To its left is a cup drawer with cork inserts; to its right is a small pull-out for grounds and a sealed drawer for whole beans. The dad of the family, who became serious about coffee in London, asked for the tambour because he did not want to look at the machine when he was reading the paper at the breakfast table eight feet away. The mother asked for the fluted glass because she did not want to see the labels on her own pantry.

The build took seven weeks because we built it as a single piece in the shop, delivered it in six sections, and set it in two days. The first morning the family used it, the dad made coffee, slid the tambour shut, and pronounced the pantry, his exact phrasing, finally English again.

Three finishes that read as the original house.

Paint-Grade Inset Casework
Hard-maple frame and door faces, sprayed in three coats of Benjamin Moore Cloud White, soft-close inset hinges.
N° 02
Fluted Reeded Glass
Half-inch reeded glass uppers, vertically run, set in painted hardwood mullions.
N° 03
Polished Nickel Pulls
Solid bar pulls in polished nickel from Armac Martin, sized 6, 10, and 14 inches by drawer width.
