— The Story of the Build —

The client retired from forty-one years of public-school teaching in June. By July he had drawn a rectangle on graph paper and asked us to build it where the garage used to be.

The original garage was a 1968 single-car block-and-shingle structure that had not held a car since 1996. The owner kept his snowblower in it. Everything else — the bicycles, the rakes, the lawnmower, three generations of skis — lived in a tarp lean-to against the back fence. He had decided, sensibly, that the lot was too valuable to keep parking a snowblower on it.

The garden-suite framework adopted by Toronto in 2022 allows a one-storey detached dwelling up to ten metres deep on a lot like his. We worked to a hard 720 square feet, the maximum allowed by the existing fire access from the side yard, with a 1-bed-1-bath plan, a galley kitchen, and a living-dining room oriented south to catch the winter sun. The footprint sits exactly where the old garage stood, which made the servicing trench a known quantity — sanitary and water under the existing concrete drive, hydro overhead from the same pole that fed the garage.

The exterior cladding is hard maple, milled rough on the back, run vertically in three-and-a-half-inch boards with a shadow reveal. Maple is not a typical exterior choice in this climate, and we argued against it for two weeks before the client won the argument by pointing out that he had stained the original garage door with leftover deck stain in 1996 and it still looked fine. The maple is finished in a UV-pigmented oil that we will recoat at year five and again at year ten, written into a small contract he keeps in his cabinet with the warranty package. Inside, the floors are polished concrete with radiant tubes, the walls are painted drywall, and the ceiling is left as the underside of the I-joist roof deck, sealed and clear-coated.

The first tenant is a postdoctoral researcher at U of T Scarborough who takes the campus shuttle each morning. He pays $2,650 a month. The owner uses the rent, in part, to put his three grandchildren through swimming lessons at the Y. The snowblower now lives, sensibly, in a tidy six-by-eight prefab shed at the side of the new suite.
